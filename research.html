Research

Research interests: conservation biology, ocean resilience, responses to climate change, and marine connectivity.

Projects

  1. 2025–now

    Investigating the recovery and recolonization of the sunflower sea star

    UCLA, Barber Lab · California coast

    The sunflower sea star was once a dominant predator in West Coast kelp forests until sea star wasting disease devastated its populations. My dissertation explores where the species persists today and how it might return. Using environmental DNA, I've surveyed sites from southern Oregon down to the Channel Islands in southern California, partnering with groups including the California Academy of Sciences and the Yurok Tribe, to detect where sunflower sea stars still occur.

    I pair these detections with ocean circulation models that simulate how larvae drift along the coast. Together, these tools let me test whether larvae from surviving northern populations can reach southern California, how that connectivity shifts with El Niño, the Pacific Decadal Oscillation, and upwelling, and which sites are the strongest candidates for reintroduction, helping inform restoration efforts in collaboration with the Sunflower Star Laboratory.

    This work is supported by the Association of Zoos & Aquariums' Saving Animals From Extinction (SAFE) sunflower sea star program. I also built Pycno Tracker, an internal tool that maps sunflower sea star sightings from iNaturalist and GBIF alongside our sampling sites, and I'm currently working with The Nature Conservancy to visualize Pycnopodia presence alongside Vibrio abundance.

  2. 2025–2026

    Monitoring marine megafauna and coral reef ecosystems

    NSF International Research Experiences for Students · Costa Rica

    With Dr. Laura May-Collado (University of Vermont) and the University of Costa Rica, I monitored marine megafauna and coral reefs using acoustic tagging, SCUBA surveys, passive acoustics, and coral gardening. I returned in 2026 as a field teaching assistant, where I designed and led an environmental DNA workshop that introduced students to eDNA sampling, laboratory workflows, and its applications for marine monitoring.

  3. 2024–2026

    Characterizing coral reef biodiversity

    Love the Oceans · Guinjata Bay, Mozambique

    I worked to assess coral cover, fish diversity, and the effects of fishing practices to inform the creation of a locally managed marine protected area in southern Mozambique.

  4. 2024–now

    Using environmental DNA to assess biodiversity and connectivity

    ARMS Restore, Reef Doctor & IH.SM · Ranobe Bay, Madagascar

    With Dr. Aaron Hartmann (Harvard) and partners at the Institut Halieutique et des Sciences Marines, I use eDNA metabarcoding to survey fish communities and ecological connectivity across coral reef, mangrove, and seagrass habitats, informing artificial reef restoration in southwestern Madagascar.

  5. 2021–2023

    Analyzing coral growth across a nitrate pollution regime

    UCSB, Stier Lab (Ocean Recoveries Lab) · Mo'orea, French Polynesia

    I assessed how the symbiotic relationship between humbug damselfish and their host corals changes across a nitrate pollution gradient.

  6. 2022

    Measuring coral regeneration and growth trade-offs

    UCSB, Stier Lab (Ocean Recoveries Lab) · Mo'orea, French Polynesia

    I investigated energetic trade-offs between coral growth, tissue regeneration, and survival after physical wounding.

  7. 2021–2022

    Observing how rising temperatures impact insect emergence

    UCSB, Young Lab · Sierra Nevada, California

    Many insects spend their early lives underwater before emerging as winged adults, and the timing of that emergence is closely tied to water temperature. I analyzed emergence patterns across Sierra Nevada mountain lakes to understand how warming waters may shift when and how many insects emerge, with ripple effects for the birds, bats, and other animals that depend on them for food.

  8. 2019–2020

    Building STEM belonging in young girls

    UCSB Curie-osity Project, Gevirtz School of Education

    We designed and tested activities to improve memory retention, participation, and interest in science among a cohort of elementary school girls.
