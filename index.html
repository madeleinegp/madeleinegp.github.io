Madeleine Pacheco

Hi! I'm a 4th-year PhD candidate and NSF Graduate Research Fellow in the Barber Lab at UCLA. I'm a marine conservation biologist who uses environmental DNA, field surveys, and ocean modeling to understand how marine communities are structured, connected, and recover from disturbance. My work spans tropical lagoons and coral reefs in Madagascar to kelp forests along the California coast, with the goal of producing science that can guide conservation and restoration.

I also work to build a more inclusive scientific community, with equitable participation of underrepresented groups in the environmental and life sciences.

Current work: the return of the sunflower sea star

The sunflower sea star (Pycnopodia helianthoides) was once a dominant predator in West Coast kelp forests. Sea star wasting disease, beginning in 2013, drove declines of more than 90% across much of its range, and it is now listed as critically endangered by the IUCN Red List.

Because the species is now rare and difficult for divers to find, I use environmental DNA (the traces of genetic material organisms shed into seawater) to identify signatures of Pycnopodia presence where visual surveys come up empty, and to detect early signs of recovery across the coast.

My dissertation asks where the species still persists along the California coast, whether larvae from surviving populations can naturally recolonize the south, and where reintroductions would do the most good.

More about this project
